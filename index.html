The Problem

Publishers are destroying games consumers paid for

When video game publishers shut down the servers required to play a game — or remove the digital rights management (DRM) software that allows it to run — they permanently destroy access to a product consumers have legitimately purchased.

This practice has no equivalent in any other industry. If the car you bought stopped working because the manufacturer decided to shut down a remote server, there would be legal consequences. That is precisely what is happening to video games, and currently there is no legal recourse.

Games that cost consumers money — sometimes full retail price — are rendered permanently inaccessible, often with minimal warning. The content consumers believed they owned simply ceases to exist.

What We Are Asking For

A clear, reasonable demand

  • 01

    Leave Games in a Playable State

    Publishers must ensure purchased games remain functional after end of service — through patches, server emulation, peer-to-peer support, or other technical means.

  • 02

    Advance Notice of Shutdown

    Consumers must receive adequate warning before a game is disabled, with sufficient time to seek alternatives or claim refunds.

  • 03

    Legislative Protection

    Laws must be enacted to enforce these obligations and provide clear recourse when publishers violate consumer rights.

Take Action

The European Commission must now respond

With over one million signatures gathered across the European Union, the Stop Killing Games European Citizens' Initiative has crossed its threshold. The European Commission is now legally required to formally address our demands.
