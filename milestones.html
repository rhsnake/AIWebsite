Timeline

Major Milestones

From a single creator's call to action to a formal European Citizens' Initiative with over one million signatures — the journey of Stop Killing Games, in order.

2023 Global — Online

Public Campaign Launched

Ross Scott, creator of the long-running YouTube machinima series Freeman's Mind and Civil Protection, begins publicly documenting and challenging the practice of video game publishers destroying games that consumers have purchased. Through detailed video essays and open letters, the campaign takes shape as a structured consumer rights argument rather than a simple community complaint.

Scott's approach is methodical: cataloguing affected titles, articulating the legal inconsistency between video games and other consumer products, and building a case for legislative intervention.

January 2024 European Union

European Citizens' Initiative Opens for Signatures

The European Citizens' Initiative titled "Securing video games for consumers after end of service" officially opens for signature collection across all 27 EU member states. Under EU law, an ECI that reaches one million signatures from a sufficient number of member states requires the European Commission to formally consider and respond to the proposal.

The initiative sets a 12-month collection window. The response from the gaming community is immediate and significant, far exceeding early projections.

2024 United Kingdom

UK Equivalent Campaign Launched

Following the momentum generated by the European initiative, a parallel consumer rights campaign launches in the United Kingdom. Operating through UK-specific petition mechanisms, the campaign extends the initiative's reach beyond the European Union and demonstrates the global scope of the problem.

The UK campaign targets British legislators and consumer protection bodies, making the argument that post-Brexit consumer rights frameworks should include explicit protections for digital game purchases.

October 2024 European Union — 27 Member States

One Million EU Signatures Crossed

The Stop Killing Games European Citizens' Initiative surpasses one million verified signatures across EU member states — a landmark achievement reached in under nine months, well ahead of the 12-month deadline. The threshold is crossed with qualifying numbers reached in multiple EU countries, satisfying the geographical distribution requirements of the ECI process.

The achievement is notable not only for its speed but for the breadth of signatories: the campaign draws support from gamers of all demographics across the continent, demonstrating that the issue transcends age, platform preference, and gaming genre.

Late 2024 Brussels, Belgium

ECI Formally Submitted to the European Commission

With the signature threshold met, the completed European Citizens' Initiative is formally submitted to the European Commission. Under the ECI Regulation, the Commission is legally obligated to examine the initiative and publish a formal communication setting out its conclusions and the actions it intends to take, or why it does not intend to act.

This marks a decisive shift: Stop Killing Games moves from a public awareness campaign into the formal legislative process of the European Union.

2025 — Ongoing Brussels, Belgium

European Commission Formal Review Period

The European Commission enters its formal six-month review period. The organisers of the ECI are entitled to a meeting with Commission representatives, and the initiative may be heard before the European Parliament. Public hearings and policy discussions are underway.

The outcome of this process could result in proposed legislation, a commitment to further study, or a formal explanation of inaction — all of which are public record and subject to democratic scrutiny.
